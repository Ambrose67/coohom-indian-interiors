# Wardrobes and lofts (Indian homes)

All sizes in mm. **(rule of thumb)** = practice convention; **(check catalogue)** = depends on hardware.

## 1. Standard sizes

| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| Overall depth (hinged) | 600 (550 minimum clear for hangers) |
| Overall depth (sliding) | about 650 — add room for the track (rule of thumb; check catalogue) |
| Main wardrobe height | 2100 (7 ft) standard; 2400 when the ceiling is 10 ft or more and a loft would otherwise be too tall |
| Hinged shutter width | 450–600 each; wider than about 600 tends to sag and needs more swing space (rule of thumb) |
| Sliding shutter width | 2 or 3 panels per wardrobe, roughly 750–1200 each (check catalogue for the track's max panel size and weight) |
| Clear space in front | 900 for hinged (shutter + standing); less for sliding |
| Carcass | 18 mm ply/HDHMR; back 6–8 mm; MR grade inside dry rooms, BWP on damp external walls or in Kerala's humidity |

## 2. Internal layout

Each bay (usually two shutters, 900–1200 wide) gets one of these:

| Zone | Size | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Long hanging (saris, salwar, dresses, kurtas) | 1400–1500 clear below the rod | Many families also keep saris folded on shelves or in a saree pull-out — ask |
| Short hanging (shirts, tops) | 900–1000 clear below the rod | Two short-hanging rods can stack in a 2100 bay for kids |
| Hanging rod height | about 1600–1750 for the main user | Lower for children and elders |
| Shelves | 300–350 apart | Top daily-use shelf at or below about 1800 |
| Drawers | internal drawers with fronts 150–250 high (rule of thumb) | Telescopic or tandem slides; put them at 300–900 above floor so they're easy to see into |
| Locker | small lockable drawer or box, usually behind a shutter at 900–1200 height (rule of thumb) | Or space for a digital safe — check the safe's size and fix it to the carcass/wall |
| Accessories | trouser pull-out, tie/belt rack, saree pull-out, jewellery tray, mirror shutter, LED with door sensor | All (check catalogue) for minimum bay width/depth |

Typical bay plan for a 4-shutter (about 1800–2400 wide) master wardrobe (rule of thumb):
1. Long hanging bay.
2. Short hanging over 2–3 drawers with a locker.
3. Shelves (folded clothes, saris), maybe a mirror shutter outside.

For 3 shutters: long hanging (1 shutter) + short hanging/drawers (1) + shelves (1). For 2 shutters: one hanging (long or short over drawers) + one shelves.

## 3. Lofts by ceiling height

Ceiling heights: **9 ft = 2743**, **10 ft = 3048**, **11 ft = 3353** (floor to slab soffit; deduct false ceiling or beam drop if the loft stops under it).

Rules (rule of thumb):
- A loft is for rarely used items (suitcases, bedding, festival items). Plan a step stool.
- Useful loft height 450–900. Under about 450 it is hard to use; over about 900 it becomes a deep, unreachable void — raise the wardrobe to 2400 or split into two loft rows.
- Align loft shutter joints with the wardrobe shutters below.

| Ceiling | Recommended | Loft height (approx) |
|---|---|---|
| 9 ft (2743) | wardrobe 2100 + one loft | about 640 |
| 10 ft (3048) | wardrobe 2400 + one loft (or 2100 + loft if the client wants a lower wardrobe and accepts a deep loft) | about 650 (or about 950) |
| 11 ft (3353) | wardrobe 2400 + two loft rows, or wardrobe 2400 + one loft to about 950 if they accept it | about 475 + 475, or about 950 |

The `wardrobe_layout` MCP tool applies these rules and returns the module plan.

## 4. RCC (concrete) lofts

Many Tamil Nadu and Kerala homes have cast-in RCC loft slabs, usually at lintel level over wardrobes, kitchens and passages. Measure each one: bottom height from floor, depth from the wall, length, and any beam or chajja behind it. Depths and heights vary from house to house, so never assume.

How to finish them:
1. **Check for damp**: RCC lofts on external walls often get seepage; fix it and apply a primer/waterproofing before closing them up. Use BWP ply wherever wood touches concrete (rule of thumb).
2. **Shutters only (economical)**: screw an 18 mm ply frame (stiles and rails) to the slab edge and walls with rawl plugs; hinge or lift-up shutters on the frame; paint or line the inside.
3. **Lined box (cleaner)**: build a thin ply box inside the RCC loft (base 6–12 mm on battens, sides as needed), then shutters. Costs more but keeps dust and moisture away from stored items.
4. **Wardrobe below**: make the wardrobe height = loft bottom minus a small scribe gap; if the wardrobe is deeper than the loft, add a fascia or bring the loft shutters forward on a frame so all shutters sit in one plane.
5. Keep shutter widths matched to the wardrobe below for a single-elevation look.

## 5. Sliding vs hinged

| Choose hinged when | Choose sliding when |
|---|---|
| There is about 900 clear in front | The bed or another door is too close for a swing |
| Full access to every bay at once is wanted | Wide shutters / a clean modern look is wanted |
| Budget is tight (hinges are cheaper than tracks) | The wardrobe is long (each panel spans 2+ bays) |
| Many internal accessories (pull-outs clash less with hinged fronts) | Plan internal drawers so they clear the inner panel (check catalogue) |

## 6. Checks
- Shutter swing clears the bed, door and switchboards.
- Hanging zones match the family (saris, school uniforms, office wear).
- Loft heights 450–900; deep RCC lofts surveyed and damp-checked.
- Wardrobe lights and sockets planned before the wall is finished.
