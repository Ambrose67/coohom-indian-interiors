# Indian modular kitchen — design rules

All sizes in mm. **(rule of thumb)** = practice convention; **(check catalogue)** = depends on brand/model.

## 1. Inputs to collect first
- Internal room length × width after plaster, and which walls are free (doors, windows, sill heights, beams, ducts).
- Main cook's height, number of people cooking, family size, cooking style (daily frying, tempering and grinding are normal; plan a chimney and a utility area).
- Appliances: fridge (single door / double door / side-by-side and its exact width), microwave or OTG, built-in oven, dishwasher, RO purifier, mixie, wet grinder, induction, cylinder or piped gas.
- Vastu: does the family want the hob in the south-east and the cook facing east?
- Is there a separate utility / work area (very common in Tamil Nadu and Kerala)?

## 2. Layout choice by room size (rule of thumb)

Counters are 600 deep. The aisle in front of a counter is 1000–1200 (1200 if two people cook together). An aisle wider than about 1500 adds walking without adding work space (rule of thumb).

| Clear internal width of the kitchen | Typical layout |
|---|---|
| under 1600 | **Straight** (one wall) only; 600 counter + ≥ 1000 aisle |
| 1600 – 2199 | **Straight**, or **L** if a second wall is free for at least 1200 |
| 2200 – 2399 | **Parallel** (600 + 1000 aisle + 600) or **L** |
| 2400 – 3399 | **L** or **U** (U needs three free walls and ≥ 1200 between the two legs) or **parallel** with a 1200 aisle |
| 3400 and above with length ≥ 3400 | **L/U + island** possible — only if the island gets ≥ 1000 clear on every working side |

Island check (hard rule): `room width ≥ counter depth + 1000 + island depth + 1000` (and the same along the length with the island length). Island depth 900 minimum for a worktop island, 1200 if it has seating on one side (rule of thumb). If the check fails, omit the island and offer a peninsula or breakfast ledge instead.

Other considerations:
- A door on a wall kills that wall for base units; a window above the counter is fine (put the sink there), but keep the window sill at or above about 1050 so the counter and backsplash fit under it (rule of thumb).
- Parallel kitchens work well where one side is the "wet" side (sink, dishwasher) and the other the "cooking" side (hob, chimney).
- In Kerala and large TN homes, a "show kitchen" (clean, open to dining) plus a back "work kitchen" is a common brief.

## 3. Heights and depths

| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| Counter height | 820–860 for most Indian cooks; set from the main cook's height (below) |
| Counter depth | 600 (650 with a back ledge); carcass about 560–580 so the top finishes at 600 |
| Base carcass | about 720 high + legs/skirting 100–150 + counter 20–40 |
| Wall units | 600–720 high, 300–350 deep, bottom 600–750 above the counter |
| Chimney | 650–750 above the hob (check catalogue for the model's minimum) |
| Tall units | about 2100–2150 high, 560–600 deep; tops line up with the wall units |
| Kitchen sockets | about 1100 above floor (over the backsplash) |

**Counter height from the cook's height** (rule of thumb from Indian anthropometry): standing elbow height ≈ 0.617 × stature; counter ≈ elbow height − 75 to 100. The MCP `kitchen_layout` tool uses elbow − 100, rounded to 5 mm and limited to 820–900. Examples: cook 1525 → about 840; 1600 → about 885; 1650 or taller → 900. If two cooks differ a lot, set for the main cook, or drop the hob section by using a thinner counter and taller legs elsewhere.

## 4. Placement

- **Work triangle** sink–hob–fridge: each leg 1200–2700, total 4000–7900. If the total is under 4000 the kitchen is cramped; over 7900 means too much walking.
- **Sink**: under or near the window (light, ventilation); single bowl with drainboard is common; BWP ply carcass, no back panel or a removable one for plumbing; RO purifier on the wall beside it with a socket and inlet.
- **Hob**: not under a window (wind blows out the flame and curtains are a fire risk), not right next to the fridge (heat) or the sink (splashing). Keep counter landing space on both sides of the hob — at least 300 (rule of thumb); one side ≥ 450 is better.
- **Vastu option**: hob in the south-east corner zone with the cook facing east; sink/water towards the north-east; keep hob and sink apart (fire/water). Offer as an option, show the trade-offs, let the client decide.
- **Fridge**: at the end of a run, near the entrance, with side and top ventilation gaps as per the fridge manual (check catalogue). Leave its full door swing clear.
- **Chimney**: centred on the hob, width equal to or more than the hob width; route the duct to an outside wall by the shortest path with as few bends as possible.
- **Microwave / OTG**: in a tall unit with the opening at about counter to chest height so hot food is not lifted overhead (rule of thumb); own socket inside the unit.
- **Mixie / wet grinder**: counter space of about 600 with sockets on the backsplash; a tilting wet grinder often lives in the utility area.
- **Gas cylinder**: ventilated base unit (no back panel, louvred or open shutter, floor-level base so the cylinder is not lifted over a step), not sealed, not directly under the hob; or outside with piped supply. Follow the gas company's rules.

## 5. Unit catalogue (standard module widths)

Base unit widths 300, 450, 600, 900 (+ corner units). Common uses:

| Unit | Width | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Sink base | 900 (single bowl + drainboard); 800–1000 range | BWP ply; hinged shutters; under-sink bin / detergent pull-out (check catalogue) |
| Hob base | 900 under a 3–4 burner hob; 600–750 for 2 burners | Drawers below (pans, kadai, pressure cooker) — not a shutter |
| Oil / bottle pull-out | cabinet width 150, 200 or 300 | Beside the hob. Hettich, Vauth-Sagel and Kaff list pull-outs for 150/200/300 cabinets (check catalogue) |
| Drawer / basket unit ("GTPT"-type tandem and basket systems) | 450, 600, 900 | Cutlery tray, thali basket, plate basket, cup-and-saucer basket, plain baskets, deep pot drawer. | Corner unit | 900–1000 along one leg (blind corner) or 900 × 900 (carousel) | Options: plain blind corner with shelf, magic corner, LeMans/kidney trays, carousel (check catalogue for minimum cabinet sizes) |

## 6. How many units — starting points by home type (rule of thumb)

Start from the **base counter running length** (sum of all base runs, corners counted once). Then allocate: sink 900 → hob 900 → oil pull-outs → corner unit → drawer units → tall units → shutters for the rest. The `kitchen_layout` MCP tool does this automatically.

| Home | Typical base run | Drawer/basket units | Oil pull-outs | Tall units | Corner | Wall units |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1BHK | 2.4–3.6 m | 2 (cutlery + thali/plate) | 1 × 150 | 0–1 | 1 if L | over most of the run |
| 2BHK | 3.6–4.8 m | 3 (+ cup-saucer / pots) | 1–2 (150 + 200) | 1 (pantry or microwave) | 1 | over most of the run |
| 3BHK | 4.8–6.0 m | 3–4 | 2 | 1–2 | 1–2 | full run + chimney |
| Villa | 6.0 m + (often island + separate utility) | 4–6 | 2 | 2–3 (pantry + appliance + broom) | 1–2 | full run, glass display units optional |

These are starting points only. Final counts come from the client's vessels, how much they store, and the actual wall lengths.

## 7. Utility / work area
- Second sink (BWP base, deep bowl for big vessels), tap and floor drain.
- Space and socket for the wet grinder, washing machine (about 600 × 600 footprint — check model), and a drying or storage rack.
- Counter 600 deep; open or louvred storage for cleaning items; good cross-ventilation.
- Often the place for the gas cylinder bank and the RO's waste-water outlet.

## 8. Checks before modelling
- Island clearance ≥ 1000 on every working side — else no island.
- Work triangle 4.0–7.9 m.
- Hob not under a window; landing space each side.
- Every drawer and shutter opens without hitting a handle, the fridge door, or a wall (fillers at corners).
- Chimney duct route exists; sockets listed for every appliance; plumbing points match the sink and RO.
